# Wireless design and troubleshooting

Balance coverage, capacity, interference and authentication.

## Learn

Coverage asks whether a client can hear a signal; capacity asks whether enough airtime is available. More transmit power or more access points can worsen contention without careful channel planning.

Wi-Fi bands differ in propagation and spectrum availability. Channel width affects throughput and interference. Evaluate client capabilities, neighboring networks and physical obstacles before treating advertised link rate as application throughput.

Use supported strong authentication and encryption, separate guest access, and manage firmware. Enterprise identity integration and personal shared passwords have different operational properties.

## Worked example

A busy room has strong signal but poor throughput. Airtime contention, interference and client density are better hypotheses than weak signal alone.

## Practice safely

1. Inspect your own Wi-Fi connection’s band and link rate.
2. Compare application throughput with link rate without stressing shared networks.
3. Sketch guest versus internal access.
4. List two coverage and two capacity measurements.

## Check your understanding

Strong signal with slow traffic most directly proves which fact?

- There cannot be interference
- Signal strength alone does not guarantee capacity
- The ISP is definitely broken
- A wider channel always fixes it

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Signal strength alone does not guarantee capacity. Capacity depends on airtime, interference, clients and upstream paths.

</details>
