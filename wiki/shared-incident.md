# Incident response and evidence

Contain harm while preserving a reliable timeline.

## Learn

Preparation includes roles, communication paths, logging, backups and exercises. Detection and analysis establish whether an event is an incident and how far it extends. Treat timestamps, identities and evidence provenance carefully.

Containment limits damage. Eradication removes causes and persistence. Recovery restores trusted operation and monitors for recurrence. These stages can overlap; actions should match business impact and evidence needs.

Do not experiment on evidence or accuse people based on one alert. Preserve originals, document handling, restrict access and involve the appropriate team. Lessons learned should produce measurable improvements.

## Worked example

A suspected compromised endpoint may need network isolation while memory and disk evidence are preserved according to the organization’s process. Wiping it immediately may destroy the evidence needed to understand scope.

## Practice safely

1. Write a fictional incident timeline from five sample events.
2. Separate observations from hypotheses.
3. Draft containment and recovery criteria.
4. List evidence handling and escalation responsibilities.

## Check your understanding

Why preserve original evidence?

- To avoid all containment
- To support reliable investigation and provenance
- To make every alert a confirmed breach
- To publish it publicly

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

To support reliable investigation and provenance. Preservation supports integrity and defensible analysis.
