# Risk, threats and security controls

Choose controls from impact and likelihood, not product names.

## Learn

A threat is a potential cause of harm; a vulnerability is a weakness; risk combines likelihood and impact in a context. An asset’s business role determines what failure costs.

Controls can be preventive, detective or corrective and may be technical, administrative or physical. Defense in depth uses complementary controls; adding many copies of the same weak control does not guarantee resilience.

Risk responses include mitigation, transfer, avoidance and acceptance. Document owners and residual risk. A compliance checklist is useful evidence but not proof that all real threats are controlled.

## Worked example

A public storage bucket with sensitive records combines exposure, weak access control and high impact. First restrict unauthorized access and preserve investigation evidence; buying monitoring alone does not remove the exposure.

## Practice safely

1. Build a risk register for a fictional small business.
2. Score three risks with a stated simple scale.
3. Choose a control, owner and validation method for each.
4. Record what remains after the control.

## Check your understanding

What is residual risk?

- Risk before any controls
- A list of all software versions
- An insurance policy
- Risk remaining after controls

<details><summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

Risk remaining after controls. Controls reduce or change risk; they rarely make it disappear.

</details>
