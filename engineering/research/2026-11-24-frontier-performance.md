## High-ceiling engineering methods

- Profile full workflows first: ETW/appropriate native traces, Tracy, samply, Perfetto or existing instruments. Identify CPU, GPU, storage, allocation, lock, startup and cross-language costs; reproduce stalls and retain tails, not averages alone.
- Remove work algorithmically before micro-optimizing: exact identity indexes, incremental dirty graphs, one-pass traversal, streaming parse, batched transactions, structure-of-arrays where access patterns justify it, and content-addressed reusable intermediates. Retain all required output and error states.
- Evaluate PGO, LTO, optimized linking, build caches, SIMD and architecture-tuned kernels individually on the actual supported artifact. Profile-guided optimization must use representative training and held-out workloads; preserve reproducibility and generic-target fallback.
- Separate game rendering, simulation and asset preparation. Compare chunk rebuilds, shader pipeline creation, asset loads, tick tails and frame-time distributions in exact game/runtime/loader/driver targets; test cold startup, repeated traversal and saves. Do not treat a synthetic scene or microbench as real game proof.
- For Sims 4 large-package workloads, compare persistent metadata indexes, bounded parallel I/O, exact invalidation and asynchronous preview preparation against the current complete mod inventory. Keep DBPF/package semantics and real CAS/game acceptance. No unsupported claims that the listed Minecraft tools apply to Sims.
- Developer-loop optimizations must preserve test inventory, full diagnostics, code coverage and release artifact acceptance. Use mandatory optimization and fix-forward rules; integrate duplicate detection and documentation walkthroughs to expose wasted work. Prefer the strongest verified solution even if its runtime, hardware or license cost is higher, within authorized constraints.
